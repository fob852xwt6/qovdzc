Explanation

Visible, enlarged pores are a common cosmetic concern. Genetics, oil production, sun damage and aging can all contribute to pore dilation. While you can't change your pore count, you can improve skin texture and make pores look smaller. One promising approach is platelet-rich plasma (PRP) therapy. In this article, we'll explore how PRP for large pores works, what to expect, its safety profile and whether it may be right for you.

What Is PRP Therapy?

Platelet-rich plasma (PRP) is a concentration of your own blood platelets, rich in growth factors that promote healing, collagen production and tissue regeneration. Originally used in sports medicine and orthopedics, PRP has gained popularity in dermatology for:

Skin rejuvenation

Hair restoration

Scar improvement

When applied to the face—often in combination with microneedling—PRP aims to trigger the body's natural repair processes, improving overall texture and tone.

How PRP Works for Skin

PRP's effectiveness stems from its high levels of bioactive proteins. Here's a simplified breakdown:

Blood draw• A small sample of your blood (20–60 mL) is taken.

Centrifugation• The sample is spun to separate platelets and plasma from red blood cells.

Collection of PRP• The platelet-rich layer is drawn off for use.

Application or Injection• PRP is injected or applied topically via microneedling, creating micro-injuries that stimulate repair.

During healing, growth factors such as platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF) and transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β) encourage collagen and elastin formation. Over weeks to months, increased collagen can tighten skin around pores, making them appear smaller.

While much PRP research focuses on scar revision and overall skin rejuvenation, several studies have noted improvements in pore appearance:

A 2018 pilot study in the Journal of Cosmetic Dermatology found that PRP combined with microneedling significantly improved skin texture, including pore size, after three monthly sessions.

In a 2020 controlled trial, participants receiving PRP plus fractional laser reported better pore refinement and smoother tone compared to laser alone.

Dermatologists anecdotally report that PRP's collagen-boosting effects help remodel stretched pore walls, leading to a more refined appearance.

Why Combine PRP with Microneedling?

Microneedling alone creates tiny channels in the skin, triggering a healing response. When paired with PRP:

Growth factors deeply penetrate via the needle tracks

Inflammation is controlled by PRP's bioactive proteins

New collagen formation is enhanced, tightening skin around pores

This synergy often yields better results than either treatment alone.

What to Expect During Treatment

Consultation– Review medical history (bleeding disorders, platelet function)– Discuss realistic outcomes for pore minimization

Preparation– Topical numbing cream applied for 30–45 minutes– Blood draw and PRP preparation (15–20 minutes)

Procedure (30–45 minutes)– Microneedling device or injections deliver PRP into the dermis

Downtime– Mild redness and pinpoint bleeding for 24–48 hours– Possible slight swelling or bruising

Recovery Care– Gentle cleansing, broad-spectrum sunscreen and hydrating ointment– Avoid retinoids, exfoliants and direct sun for 3–5 days

Multiple sessions—typically 3 to 4 spaced 4 to 6 weeks apart—often yield best results. Collagen remodeling continues over 3–6 months, so improvements may be gradual.

PRP for large pores is generally well tolerated because you're using your own blood. However, any procedure carries potential risks:

Temporary bruising or swelling at injection sites

Mild redness or flaking after microneedling

Infection (rare when performed under sterile conditions)

Pain or discomfort during injections

To minimize risks, choose a board-certified dermatologist or licensed aesthetic provider with experience in PRP and microneedling.

PRP may be ideal if you:

Have moderately enlarged pores due to aging, sun damage or oiliness

Alternatives and Combination Therapies

If PRP alone doesn't fully address your concerns, consider pairing it with:

Chemical peels (salicylic or glycolic acid) to exfoliate and refine texture

Fractional lasers (e.g., CO₂ or erbium) for deeper collagen stimulation

Radiofrequency microneedling for simultaneous heating and needling

Topical retinoids to regulate cell turnover and sebum production
