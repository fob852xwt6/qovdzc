AS SOMEONE WHO'S spent the last few summers rotating through every type of warm-weather footwear, I’ve tried it all: mules, loafers, minimal sneakers, even those trendy clogs that won’t die. But I’ve come to a surprisingly simple yet conclusion: the most essential summer shoe isn’t closed at all. It’s sandals.

I know what you’re thinking, but stay with me. I’m not referring to dad shoes or slip-ons your artsy friend wore to Coachella. That kind of image is exactly why guys still underestimate sandals. But today’s options are smarter, more comfortable, and way more versatile than you’d expect. I’m not talking about those rubber pool slides or flip flops you toss in your gym bag. I mean polished, grown-up sandals with cushioned footbeds, grippy outsoles, and secure straps that actually hold up at the farmer’s market, rooftop bar, and 90-degree subway platform.

My turning point came on a long weekend in Houston. I packed light, brought one pair of suede sandals on a whim, and ended up wearing it the entire trip. It was cooler (literally) than my sneakers, didn’t rub or blister like boat shoes, and managed to make even a polo and shorts look put together. That’s when it clicked: when the temperature spikes, less is more, and the right sandals are the ultimate summer flex.

So what are the right sandals? For starters, skip anything that looks like you borrowed it from a college dorm shower. Look for pairs with a sturdy footbed, adjustable straps, and a profile that’s closer to a minimalist dress sneaker than a flip-flop. Think rugged Teva-style platforms if you’re hiking or festival-hopping, or leather options from Todd Snyder for breezy European vacations.

How to Style Sandals

Styling them is easier than you think. It’s about balance: let your feet breathe, but keep the rest of your outfit looking sharp and seasonally appropriate.

Match the Sandal to the Setting

Where you’re going should shape what you’re wearing, and what you’re wearing on your feet.

For the city

Choose sleek performance sandals (like Suicoke DEPA-Cab) in black or neutral tones. Pair it with chino shorts or summer pants and a camp-collar shirt for a clean, elevated look. You’ll want some performance features like cushioned footbeds and breathable straps to keep your feet supported and cool during hot weather commutes.

For casual settings

Whether you’re going to a backyard BBQ or camping with friends, stick with athletic-style sandals (Hoka, Teva, Merrell) that have cushioned soles and technical straps. Wear either of these with short shorts, a breathable tee, and a hat. Unlike the bulky, orthopedic-looking styles of the past, these sandals feel sleek and structured, more like footwear, less like gear. With streamlined straps, tonal colorways, and minimal branding, today’s active sandals give a cleaner silhouette that complements the crisp lines of well-cut shorts.

For vacation mode

Leather or suede slides (like Todd Snyder Crossover Strap or Birkenstock Arizona) elevate your airport-to-beach fit. Style with matching sets, linen shirts and drawstring shorts.

Slide sandals (e.g., Lululemon Restfeel, Adidas Adilette 22)

Keep it crisp with tailored shorts and a simple t-shirt. These typically work with swim trunks or board shorts.

Leather sandals (e.g., Birkenstock Milano, Cole Haan, or even rugged fisherman styles)

Great with a short-sleeve knit polo and pleated shorts. The more tailored your outfit, the classier these feel.

What to Style With Your Sandals

The bottom line is sandals have evolved from yesteryears. They’re no longer the lazy choice, they’re classy, comfortable, and let your dogs breathe. And once you find a pair that fits your lifestyle and your wardrobe, don’t be surprised if they become the only shoes you reach for until summer wraps up.
