What are the possible causes of sharp pain in the wrist joint?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Sharp wrist pain is often caused by injuries to the bones, ligaments, or tendons, or by underlying conditions such as arthritis, carpal tunnel syndrome, or other nerve-related problems. Common culprits include sprains, fractures, tendonitis, and repetitive strain injuries.

Sharp pain in the wrist can arise from various causes.

Common Causes:

Injuries: Falls or direct impacts can lead to fractures or sprains, especially affecting the bones and ligaments in the wrist.

Arthritis: Both osteoarthritis and rheumatoid arthritis can cause pain in the wrist joint due to inflammation and wear and tear.

Tendonitis: Overuse or repetitive movements can inflame the tendons, leading to pain.

Nerve Problems: Conditions like carpal tunnel syndrome involve pressure on nerves, causing pain or tingling.

Ulnar-Sided Pain:

Pain on the pinky side of the wrist can be due to specific issues like injuries to the TFCC (triangular fibrocartilage complex), which is common in athletes who use their wrists a lot.

Understanding the exact cause of wrist pain often requires a careful assessment, including medical history and sometimes imaging tests.
