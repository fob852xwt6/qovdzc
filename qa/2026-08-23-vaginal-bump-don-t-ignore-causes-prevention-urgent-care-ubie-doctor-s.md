Vaginal bumps can have several causes. The most common include Bartholin gland cysts (fluid-filled lumps near the vaginal opening), genital warts caused by HPV, ingrown hairs, sebaceous cysts, and folliculitis. Less common causes include vulvar cancer or skin conditions like lichen sclerosus. Prevention strategies include practicing good hygiene, using protection during sex, getting the HPV vaccine, and attending regular gynecological check-ups.

Explanation

Vaginal bumps can come from a few different conditions. One common cause is a Bartholin gland cyst, which happens when one of the glands near the vaginal opening gets blocked and fills with fluid. This may feel like a harmless lump but can sometimes become painful if it gets infected. Another cause is genital warts, which are small bumps caused by an infection with the human papillomavirus (HPV). These warts are usually not dangerous but can be uncomfortable and spread to other areas. There are also rarer causes of vaginal lumps, such as other unusual growths that might not fit into these categories. To help prevent some of these issues, practicing safe sex is very important. Using protection and considering an HPV vaccine can lower the risk of developing genital warts. Keeping good personal hygiene and attending regular gynecological exams can also help detect and manage any changes early.
