What are the possible causes of a hard lump on the anus, and should it be biopsied?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

A hard lump on the anus can result from several conditions, including thrombosed hemorrhoids, anal skin tags, abscesses, benign growths, or, less commonly, premalignant or malignant lesions. Because the underlying causes vary widely in severity, a healthcare provider may recommend a biopsy if the lump appears suspicious, in order to accurately determine whether it is benign or cancerous and guide appropriate treatment.

A hard lump near the anus might be a sign of different issues. Some lumps are benign, meaning they are not dangerous. However, certain causes can include premalignant changes or even cancers like squamous cell carcinoma of the anal margin. These lumps may be hard because of changes in the skin or underlying tissues. Doctors pay close attention to lumps that seem unusual, are growing, or have irregular features since they can be a sign of a serious condition.

A biopsy is a process where a small piece of tissue is removed from the lump and examined under a microscope. This test helps the doctor decide if the lump is benign or if it might be a premalignant or malignant lesion. If you notice a hard lump or any changes around the anus, you can use a free symptom checker to help identify possible causes before consulting with a healthcare professional. They can decide if a biopsy or other tests are needed to guide the best treatment and ensure your health is protected.
