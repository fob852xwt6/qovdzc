Mounjaro Sulfur Burps Checklist: Is it a Red Flag?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Sulfur burps while taking Mounjaro are a common side effect, typically caused by slowed gastric emptying, shifts in gut bacteria, and dietary changes. Most cases improve within weeks by eating smaller meals, chewing thoroughly, staying hydrated, and using over-the-counter remedies like simethicone or bismuth subsalicylate.

However, warning signs require prompt medical attention. Red flags include sulfur burps lasting longer than six weeks, severe abdominal pain, blood in stool or vomit, fever, persistent vomiting, or signs of dehydration—which may indicate gastroparesis, infection, or another serious condition.

Why Mounjaro May Cause Sulfur Burps

Mounjaro (tirzepatide) is a dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist approved for type 2 diabetes and, more recently, for chronic weight management. Common gastrointestinal side effects include nausea, vomiting, diarrhea and burping. Here's why sulfur burps can occur:

Slowed Gastric EmptyingGLP-1 agonists slow the rate at which food leaves your stomach, giving gut bacteria more time to ferment food and produce gas.

Altered Gut FloraChanges in digestive hormones may shift the balance of bacteria in your intestines, increasing hydrogen sulfide producers.

Dietary AdjustmentsYou may eat differently (e.g., increased protein for weight loss support), introducing more sulfur-rich foods (eggs, meat, cruciferous vegetables).

Checklist: Normal Side Effect vs. Red Flag

Use this checklist to gauge whether your sulfur burps are likely a routine side effect or a sign you should seek immediate medical attention.

Duration & Frequency

❏ Normal: Mild sulfur burps that occur occasionally (1–2 times/day) and improve within 2–4 weeks of dose stabilization.

⚠️ Red Flag: Burps persist beyond 6 weeks or worsen over time despite basic measures.

Associated Symptoms

❏ Normal: No other gastrointestinal issues or only mild nausea, manageable by meal timing and diet tweaks.

⚠️ Red Flag: Accompanied by severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, high fever, blood in vomit or stool, unintentional rapid weight loss.

Impact on Daily Life

❏ Normal: Occasional embarrassment but no major interference with eating or social activities.

Home Remedies & Dietary Changes

⚠️ Red Flag: No relief after 1–2 weeks of diet modifications and over-the-counter aids (eg, antacids, simethicone).

Practical Tips to Manage Sulfur Burps

Most cases respond well to lifestyle and dietary changes. Try the following strategies:

Eat smaller, more frequent meals rather than three large meals.

Chew each bite slowly to reduce swallowed air.

Limit high-sulfur foods (eggs, onions, garlic, beans, cruciferous vegetables) or introduce them gradually.

Stay hydrated—sip water throughout the day to promote digestion.

Avoid carbonated beverages and chewing gum, which increase swallowed air.

Consider taking simethicone (per label instructions) to help break down gas bubbles.
