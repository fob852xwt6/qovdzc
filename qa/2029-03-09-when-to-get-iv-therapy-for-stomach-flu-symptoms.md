Why is Rehydration Important for Recovery?

Stomach flu, or viral gastroenteritis, leads to excessive fluid loss due to vomiting and diarrhea.1 Dehydration occurs rapidly and can be life-threatening if not addressed promptly.

Losing fluids during a stomach flu episode also leads to an imbalance in essential electrolytes like sodium, potassium, and chloride.2 This imbalance can result in muscle weakness, heart irregularities, and severe cramping. To counteract this, consider the best electrolyte drink for stomach flu. These drinks are designed to restore electrolyte balance, promoting a quicker recovery.

Consuming fluids is crucial to help rehydrate the body and maintain essential functions. Sip on clear liquids such as water, herbal teas, or broths to stay hydrated. If it is difficult to keep fluids down or you are moderately or severely dehydrated, an IV treatment can help rehydrate your body quickly.

Types of Rehydration Solutions

Rehydration is a fundamental component of managing the effects of illnesses like stomach flu, which can result in significant fluid and electrolyte losses. Various rehydration solutions are available to counter these effects and facilitate a swift recovery.

Homemade Rehydration Drinks

Commercial Electrolyte Drinks

ORS is a meticulously formulated rehydration solution that consists of a precise combination of water, glucose, and electrolytes such as sodium and potassium. Glucose aids in sodium and water absorption in the small intestine.

ORS is highly effective in rehydrating individuals who have lost fluids due to conditions like the stomach flu. It helps restore electrolyte balance, promote fluid absorption, and mitigate dehydration. The World Health Organization (WHO) recognizes ORS as a crucial tool in managing diarrheal diseases, and it is often recommended for children with acute diarrhea.

ORS is typically available in pre-packaged sachets, easily mixed with clean water. It can be administered orally in small, frequent sips to avoid overwhelming the stomach. The solution’s balance of glucose and electrolytes ensures efficient absorption in the intestine.

Homemade rehydration drinks can be prepared using readily available ingredients, usually a combination of water, salt, sugar, and (optionally) citrus juice or mashed banana. These ingredients mimic the composition of commercial ORS to some extent.

While homemade rehydration drinks may not have the precise formulation of commercial ORS, they can still effectively replenish fluids and electrolytes. They are a suitable option when commercial ORS is not readily available.

To make a homemade rehydration solution, mix one liter of clean water, six teaspoons of regular sugar, and half a teaspoon of salt. It is important to ensure accurate measurements and avoid excessive salt content. This solution should be consumed in small sips as well.

Commercial electrolyte drinks are widely available in the market and are specifically formulated to mimic the electrolyte composition of body fluids. They contain a balance of sodium, potassium, chloride, and sometimes magnesium, in addition to sugars and flavorings.

Commercial electrolyte drinks are convenient and effective in rehydration. They provide a ready-made option for those who may find homemade solutions less palatable. These drinks are especially useful when dehydration is accompanied by nausea, making tolerating plain water or homemade solutions challenging.

Commercial electrolyte drinks are typically consumed as per the manufacturer’s instructions. They are available in various flavors and can be purchased over the counter at most pharmacies and convenience stores.

What Causes Food Poisoning?

Food poisoning is a blanket term used to refer to several different causes of gastrointestinal distress. There are dozens of viruses, parasites, and harmful bacteria that can cause food poisoning. In most cases, it is impossible to know what type of bacteria caused your illness without lab tests.

There are three main causes of food poisoning: bacteria, viruses, and parasites.

Viruses

Parasites

Listeria

Viruses are infectious agents and particles that cause a number of diseases. They can be passed from person to person across air, food, water, and touch. The most common viral causes of food poisoning include:

Norovirus

Rotavirus

Hepatitis A

Astrovirus

When should you see a doctor for the stomach flu?

Although most people will recover from stomach flu within a couple of days without medical attention, it’s still important to know when to visit a doctor.

It’s important to see a doctor if you:

Have been vomiting for 12 hours or more

Have had diarrhea that has not gone away after 2 days

Have a fever, which may indicate an infection is present

Have blood or pus in the stool, as this may be a sign of a different condition

Are showing signs of dehydration such as confusion, headache, fatigue, and/or dizziness or lightheadedness

All children under 3 years old who have stomach flu should be seen by a doctor
