Causes of Tiredness After 5 Minutes of Exercise

Low Levels of Glucose

Low glucose levels are one of the common causes of fatigue after only five minutes of exercise. The body uses glucose as its main energy source, which is produced when food-borne carbohydrates are broken down. For later use, the body stores glucose as glycogen in the muscles and liver. The liver stores about 400 calories of glycogen on average, while the muscles hold about 1600 calories. However, during vigorous or prolonged exercise, these glycogen stores may quickly run low, resulting in fatigue.

Simple and Complex Carbohydrates: An Overview

It’s critical to properly fuel the body if you want to keep your energy levels up during exercise. Simple carbohydrates are sugars that can be found in processed baked goods, sugary drinks, and sweets. They give you a quick energy boost when you’re working out hard. Complex carbohydrates, on the other hand, which can be found in whole grains and high-fiber fruits and vegetables, take longer to digest and are stored as glycogen, giving you steady energy for a longer period of time. Exercise-related fatigue is more likely to occur if your glycogen stores are insufficient. In order to support an active lifestyle, it is crucial to balance the simple and complex carbohydrates in your diet.

Trying Too Hard Too Soon

When beginning a fitness regimen, exercising too vigorously can quickly result in exhaustion. Without giving their bodies time to gradually adjust to the physical demands of exercise, many beginners risk pushing themselves too far. If you struggle to complete even 5 minutes of exercise, think about lowering the intensity or adding walking breaks to your schedule. Prioritize resisting the urge to compare yourself to others and steadily increasing your stamina. Another factor is mental fatigue, which can result from the brain misinterpreting the difficulty of exercise and causing an early exhaustion.

Overtraining

When people don’t include enough rest and recovery days in their training schedules, overtraining results. Without it, muscles can swell, inflame, and exhaust themselves. Rest is necessary for muscle repair and strengthening. Make sure to include rest days in your workout schedule and give your muscles enough time to recover to avoid overtraining.

Dehydration

Exercise performance can be significantly impacted by dehydration, which can also make you tired. A 10% to 20% drop in performance can result from even a 2% reduction in fluids. It’s important to drink enough water before, during, and after exercise. Men’s Health advises staying properly hydrated by consuming 8 oz (237 ml) of water an hour before working out and doubling that amount afterward.

Poor Sleep

Sleep is essential for both physical and mental recovery, which affects how well you can exercise. Muscle repair and the nighttime release of growth hormones can both be hampered by poor sleep. You might feel low energy and have less motivation to exercise as a result. Make good sleep hygiene a priority and set a goal of getting enough sleep each night to support your active lifestyle.

Possible Underlying Health Issues

Consider possible underlying health issues if you frequently feel exhausted after just a few minutes of exercise. Exercise-related fatigue can be influenced by elements such as vitamin or mineral deficiencies, long-term medical conditions, or even mental health issues like depression. It is advisable to speak with a healthcare provider if you have any concerns about your level of energy in order to determine and treat any underlying medical issues.
