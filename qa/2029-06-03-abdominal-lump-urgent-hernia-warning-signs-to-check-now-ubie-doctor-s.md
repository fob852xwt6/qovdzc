What are the signs of a hernia if I notice an abdominal lump?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

A hernia often appears as a visible lump or bulge in the abdomen or groin that may feel sore, heavy, or tender—especially when you cough, lift heavy objects, bend over, or strain. Common signs of a hernia include:

A soft lump that may disappear when lying down

Aching, burning, or dragging sensations near the bulge

Pain that worsens with activity or lifting

Redness, swelling, nausea, or vomiting (which may signal a medical emergency)

If your lump is growing, becoming painful, or accompanied by other symptoms, see a doctor promptly. Not sure if what you're feeling is a hernia or something else? Explanation

When you see a lump in your abdomen, it might be a hernia. A hernia happens when an internal part of your body pushes through a weak spot in the muscles. Common signs include a bulge that might become more obvious when you stand up, cough, or strain. The area around the lump can feel sore, tender, or heavy. In some cases, the pain may get worse with physical activity or lifting. The lump may change in size, sometimes disappearing when you lie down. In some hernias, the skin over the lump might become red or warm, which can be a sign of inflammation or that the hernia is getting trapped (strangulated). Internal hernias can be trickier to spot because they may not cause a visible bulge but can produce vague abdominal pain or discomfort. If you notice these symptoms or if the lump suddenly becomes more painful or is accompanied by nausea and vomiting, it's important to get evaluated by a healthcare provider.
