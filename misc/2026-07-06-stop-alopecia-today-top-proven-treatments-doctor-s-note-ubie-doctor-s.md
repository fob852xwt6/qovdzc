What are the most common treatments for alopecia?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Hair loss treatments depend on the cause. For pattern hair loss (androgenetic alopecia), topical minoxidil is first-line, with oral finasteride added for men. Alopecia areata is treated with intralesional or topical corticosteroids, topical immunotherapy, or oral JAK inhibitors. Telogen effluvium responds best to correcting underlying triggers—thyroid imbalance, iron deficiency, nutritional gaps, or medication side effects—sometimes paired with short-term minoxidil. Adjunct options include low-level laser therapy, platelet-rich plasma (PRP), hair transplantation, and cosmetic solutions. The right treatment depends on type, severity, and individual health factors—including dosing, side effects, and expected timelines.

Explanation

Alopecia—loosely defined as hair loss from the scalp or body—affects millions worldwide. The most common form is androgenetic alopecia (male- or female-pattern baldness), but other types such as alopecia areata (an autoimmune condition) also occur. While hair loss can be distressing, several well-studied treatments can slow, stop, or even reverse thinning in many people. Below is an overview of the most common alopecia treatments, organized by type of hair loss, with practical tips on what to expect and when to seek professional help.

When to See a DoctorYou should speak to a healthcare professional if you experience:

Sudden, extensive hair loss

Scalp pain, redness, scaling or pustules

Signs of systemic illness (fever, weight loss)

Nail changes (pitting, ridges) with hair loss (suggestive of autoimmune disease)

Emerging and Future TherapiesResearch continues into novel agents for alopecia treatment, including:

Oral and topical JAK inhibitors for alopecia areata

Wnt pathway modulators to stimulate follicle regeneration

Stem cell–derived therapies

ConclusionAlopecia treatment depends on the specific type and severity of hair loss. Androgenetic alopecia responds well to topical minoxidil and oral finasteride in men, while alopecia areata often requires immune-modulating approaches like corticosteroids or JAK inhibitors. For diffuse shedding, correcting nutritional deficiencies and minimizing stress can be key. Cosmetic options help bridge the gap while medical therapies take effect.

Always remember:

Early intervention often yields better results.

Response to treatment varies—patience is essential.

Regular follow-up with your dermatologist or primary care provider ensures safe, effective care.
