What Is a PRP (Vampire) Facial?

A PRP facial involves these basic steps:

Blood draw: A small vial of your blood is collected.

Centrifugation: The blood is spun to separate red cells from platelet-rich plasma.

Microneedling or injection: The PRP (rich in growth factors) is applied via tiny micro-injuries or injections to the face.

Healing response: Your body's natural repair mechanisms activate, stimulating collagen and elastin production.

By harnessing your own growth factors, a PRP facial offers a more "natural" alternative to synthetic fillers or chemicals.

Preparing for Your PRP Facial

Consultation

Discuss medical history, current medications (especially blood thinners), and desired outcomes.

Mention any history of cold sores—antiviral medication may be prescribed to prevent an outbreak.

Avoid certain products

Stop retinoids and alpha-hydroxy acids (AHAs) 3–5 days before treatment.

Skip blood-thinning supplements like fish oil or high-dose vitamin E for 1 week prior (with your doctor's approval).

Stay hydrated and nourished

Drink plenty of water and eat a balanced meal before your session.

Well-hydrated skin responds better to treatment.

Post-Treatment Care

Gentle cleansing: Use a mild, fragrance-free cleanser for 3 days.

Moisturize: Apply a soothing, hydrating cream to support barrier repair.

Sun protection: Broad-spectrum SPF 30+ is a must; avoid direct sun for at least one week.

No makeup: Wait 24–48 hours before applying cosmetics to reduce infection risk.

Is a PRP Facial Right for You?

Consider a PRP facial if you want:

A natural, autologous (self-derived) treatment.

Gradual, long-lasting improvement rather than an instant "fill."

Minimal downtime with low risk of allergic reaction.

Keep in mind:

Cost per session can range from $500 to $1,500 depending on location and provider expertise.

Multiple sessions (typically 3–4, spaced 4–6 weeks apart) deliver the best results.

Maintenance treatments once or twice a year help preserve gains.

Speak to a Doctor

While PRP facials are safe for most people, you should always speak to a doctor if you have:

Bleeding disorders or are on anticoagulant therapy.
