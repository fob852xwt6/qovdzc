What to Consider

Insulation

Fill Power

If you’re set on genuine down (goose or duck), “fill power” is the key number to know. Technically, it measures the number of cubic inches one ounce of down can fill. Most puffer jackets range from 450 to 900 fill power, with 800+ being premium. Jackets with higher fill power offer maximum warmth without bulk, though they often come with a a higher price tag.

Down vs. Synthetic

The difference between down and synthetic insulation lies within their materials and performance. Down, made from goose or duck plumage, offers unbeatable warmth-to-weight ratio, compressibility, and long-lasting durability—but it struggles to keep you warm when wet. Synthetic insulation, on the other hand, is made from man-made fibers designed to mimic down's warmth while retaining heat even in damp conditions.

Weather Resistance

Water Resistance

A durable water-repellent (DWR) coating is essential for light rain or snow. It keeps you dry and comfortable when the weather takes a turn.

Windproofing

A windproof design acts as a shield, blocking those icy gusts and keeping your body heat locked in where it belongs. This feature is particularly important for high-altitude adventures or city commutes on breezy winter days.

Fit

You’ll typically come across a few different fits to consider, depending on your needs and style preferences.

Slim Fit

A sleek, more tailored look that hugs the body without being restrictive. This is a great choice if you want a streamlined look and don’t plan on layering heavily underneath. It’s also ideal for urban environments where style is key.

Regular Fit

This is the most versatile option, offering enough room for layering without being too loose or too tight. It provides a good balance between comfort, mobility, and warmth.

Relaxed Fit

This fit is roomier and provides plenty of space for layering thick hoodies or fleece beneath your jacket. It’s also ideal for those who prefer a more laid-back, comfortable fit, but keep in mind it might not be as flattering as a slim fit. However, not all are created to be bulky.

Weight & Packability

You should consider a lightweight puffer jacket that won't weigh you down, whether you're trekking up the mountain or dashing to catch the subway. Packability is the cherry on top, especially for travelers or anyone short on storage space. Many puffer jackets can compress into their own pocket or tiny pouch, making them easy to stash in a backpack, carry-on, or even a glove compartment.

Features

The best puffer jackets come loaded with features that make winter easy to withstand including adjustable hoods, hand-warmer pockets, hems and cuffs that cinch shut to keep the cold at bay.
