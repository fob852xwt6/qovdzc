What long-term solutions exist to fix restless leg syndrome?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Restless leg syndrome (RLS) is a chronic neurological condition, and long-term management typically requires a combination of treatments tailored to symptom severity.

Common long-term solutions for RLS include:

Gabapentin enacarbil – an anticonvulsant that reduces nerve-related discomfort and is often a first-line option.

Dopamine agonists – such as pramipexole, ropinirole, and rotigotine, which help regulate dopamine signaling linked to RLS symptoms.

Iron supplementation – recommended when ferritin levels are low, a common underlying contributor.

Lifestyle adjustments – including regular exercise, improved sleep hygiene, and reducing caffeine, alcohol, and nicotine.

Opioids or benzodiazepines – occasionally prescribed for severe, treatment-resistant cases.

Explanation

Restless leg syndrome (RLS) is a condition that causes an uncontrollable urge to move the legs, often leading to discomfort, especially at night. Long-term solutions focus on managing symptoms effectively.

Medications: Dopamine agonists, such as pramipexole, are commonly used for long-term management. They help reduce symptoms by acting on dopamine pathways in the brain. Studies show that pramipexole can be effective over many years, although some people may experience side effects or need dose adjustments. Other common medications include gabapentin and benzodiazepams.

Regular Monitoring: It is important for individuals using medications like dopamine agonists to have regular follow-ups with their healthcare providers to monitor effectiveness and adjust treatment as needed.

Lifestyle Adjustments: In addition to medication, lifestyle changes such as maintaining a regular sleep schedule, exercising, and avoiding caffeine and alcohol can also help manage RLS symptoms.

By combining medication with lifestyle changes, many people find significant relief from the symptoms of RLS over the long term.
