What Is PRP and How It Works

PRP stands for Platelet-Rich Plasma, a concentration of your own blood platelets. Platelets release growth factors that can:

Stimulate dormant hair follicles

Increase blood supply to the brow area

Improve thickness and density over time

The PRP Procedure

Blood draw: A small sample is taken from your arm.

Centrifugation: Blood is spun to separate platelets from other components.

Injection: The platelet-rich plasma is carefully injected into targeted brow areas.

Each session typically takes 30–45 minutes. Most protocols recommend 3–4 treatments spaced 4–6 weeks apart, with maintenance sessions every 6–12 months.

Lifestyle and Home Care Tips

Alongside medical treatments, simple daily habits can support healthier brows:

Gentle cleansing: Avoid harsh soaps or scrubs on the brow area.

Avoid over-tweezing: Follow your natural brow shape and let hairs grow in between shaping sessions.

Balanced diet: Emphasize lean protein, healthy fats, fruits, and vegetables.

Sun protection: Apply a soft sunscreen or wear a hat to shield delicate skin.

Stress management: Chronic stress can worsen hair-loss conditions; practice relaxation techniques.

Potential Side Effects and Considerations

Any treatment comes with possible downsides. For PRP:

Mild pain or bruising at injection sites

Temporary swelling or redness

Rare infection (minimized by sterile technique)

For topical or prescription options:

Scalp/minoxidil crossover may irritate eyelids

Bimatoprost can alter eye color in rare cases

Steroids risk skin thinning if overused

A frank discussion with your doctor about risks versus benefits will ensure you choose the safest, most effective path.
