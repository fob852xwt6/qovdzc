# Eye Twitching in Pregnancy: Don't Ignore This Vital Warning Sign | Ubie Doctor's Note

Published on: 4/13/2026

Eye Twitching in Pregnancy: Is It Stress, Fatigue, or a Magnesium Deficiency?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Eyelid twitching during pregnancy—whether in the left or right eye—is usually harmless. The most common causes include stress, fatigue, poor sleep, eye strain, mild dehydration, and caffeine intake. Magnesium deficiency can also contribute, but it should be confirmed by a healthcare provider before starting any supplements.

Simple relief steps often help, but certain red flags require prompt medical attention: twitching that lasts for weeks, spreads to other facial muscles, causes vision changes, or occurs alongside signs of preeclampsia (severe headache, sudden swelling, visual disturbances, or high blood pressure).

Explanation

Eye twitching during pregnancy can feel strange and distracting. If you've noticed left eye twitching (or twitching in either eye), you're not alone. Many pregnant women experience this at some point.

In most cases, eye twitching is harmless. But it's still important to understand why it happens — especially during pregnancy, when your body is already under extra strain.

Let's break down the common causes, when to seek help, and what you can do about it.

What Is Eye Twitching?

Eye twitching, medically called myokymia, is a small, involuntary spasm of the eyelid muscle. It usually affects:

The lower eyelid

One eye (often the left eye twitching, though either side can be affected)

Episodes lasting a few seconds to a few minutes

Repeated spasms over days or weeks

It is rarely painful. More often, it feels like a flutter, pull, or slight jerk.

In pregnancy, eye twitching is typically linked to everyday factors like stress, fatigue, or nutritional changes.

Common Causes of Eye Twitching in Pregnancy

1. Stress

Pregnancy is exciting, but it can also be stressful — physically and emotionally.

Hormonal changes, sleep disruption, work concerns, and preparing for a baby all increase stress levels. Stress stimulates the nervous system, which can trigger small muscle spasms, including in the eyelids.

If your left eye twitching seems worse during busy or anxious days, stress is a likely contributor.

Signs stress may be the cause:

Twitching comes and goes

It worsens during emotional strain

You also feel tense or overwhelmed

It improves with rest or relaxation

2. Fatigue and Lack of Sleep

Fatigue is one of the most common reasons for eye twitching — pregnant or not.

During pregnancy, your body is:

Growing a baby

Increasing blood volume

Adjusting hormones

Working harder overall

Sleep can also be disrupted by nausea, frequent urination, discomfort, or anxiety.

When you're overtired, your muscles and nerves become more irritable. This can easily trigger eyelid spasms.

If you're experiencing left eye twitching along with exhaustion, fatigue is a strong possibility.

3. Magnesium Deficiency

Magnesium plays an important role in:

Muscle relaxation

Nerve function

Electrolyte balance

Low magnesium levels can cause muscle twitching, including eyelid spasms.

Pregnancy increases your body's need for magnesium. If your intake doesn't keep up, you may notice:

Muscle cramps

Leg spasms

Restlessness

Eye twitching

However, mild eyelid twitching alone does not automatically mean you have a deficiency. Blood testing is the only reliable way to confirm low magnesium.

Never start high-dose supplements without speaking to your doctor. Too much magnesium can also cause problems.

4. Caffeine Intake

If you rely on coffee to get through pregnancy fatigue, take note: caffeine can overstimulate nerves and muscles.

Even moderate caffeine may contribute to:

Left eye twitching

Sleep disturbance

Pregnancy guidelines generally recommend limiting caffeine to 200 mg per day (about one 12-ounce cup of coffee).

5. Dehydration

Pregnant women need more fluids. Even mild dehydration can make muscles more prone to twitching.

If you also notice:

Dark urine

Headache

Increasing fluid intake may help reduce twitching episodes.

6. Eye Strain

Screen time, reading, and phone use can strain eye muscles. Pregnancy-related dry eyes can make this worse.

Digital strain may trigger:

One-sided twitching (such as left eye twitching)

Blurry vision

Eye discomfort

Taking regular breaks from screens can reduce symptoms.

When Is Eye Twitching Not Normal?

Most pregnancy-related eye twitching is harmless and temporary.

However, you should speak to a doctor if:

The twitching lasts more than a few weeks

The eyelid fully closes during spasms

Other parts of your face start twitching

You develop facial weakness

What You Can Do to Reduce Eye Twitching

The good news: simple lifestyle adjustments often improve symptoms.

✅ Prioritize Sleep

Aim for 7–9 hours per night

Use pregnancy pillows for comfort

Create a consistent bedtime routine

✅ Manage Stress

Try gentle prenatal yoga

Practice breathing exercises

Take short daily walks

Talk openly about pregnancy worries

Stress reduction often decreases nervous system irritation.

✅ Stay Hydrated

Drink water regularly throughout the day

Monitor urine color (light yellow is ideal)

✅ Check Your Diet

Magnesium-rich foods include:

Leafy greens (spinach, kale)

Nuts (almonds, cashews)

Seeds (pumpkin seeds)

Whole grains

Beans

Do not start supplements unless your doctor recommends them.

✅ Reduce Caffeine

Gradually cut back if needed

Replace with herbal teas approved for pregnancy

✅ Limit Screen Time

Follow the 20-20-20 rule (every 20 minutes, look 20 feet away for 20 seconds)

Use artificial tears if pregnancy-related dry eye occurs

Why Is It Often the Left Eye?

Many people specifically notice left eye twitching, but there is no medical reason the left side is more significant than the right.

Twitching can happen on either side depending on:

Muscle fatigue

Nerve sensitivity

Sleeping position

Eye strain patterns

The side itself usually does not indicate a serious issue.

Is Eye Twitching a Sign of a Serious Pregnancy Problem?

In almost all cases, no.

Eye twitching is not typically linked to:

Miscarriage

Preeclampsia

Gestational diabetes

Fetal problems

However, if eye twitching occurs alongside:

Severe headache

Sudden swelling

Vision disturbances (like flashing lights or blurring)

High blood pressure

Seek medical care immediately. These could signal preeclampsia, which requires urgent evaluation.

How Long Does It Last?

Most cases of pregnancy-related eye twitching:

Last a few days to a few weeks

Come and go

Resolve after stress improves

Often disappear postpartum

If it persists beyond several weeks, it's reasonable to speak with your healthcare provider for further evaluation.

When to Speak to a Doctor

You should speak to a doctor if:

Twitching is persistent or worsening

It interferes with vision

It spreads to other facial muscles

You suspect nutritional deficiencies

The Bottom Line

Eye twitching in pregnancy — including left eye twitching — is most often caused by:

Stress

Mild dehydration

Caffeine

Possible magnesium imbalance

In the vast majority of cases, it is temporary and harmless.

Simple steps like improving sleep, managing stress, staying hydrated, and reviewing your diet often resolve the issue.
