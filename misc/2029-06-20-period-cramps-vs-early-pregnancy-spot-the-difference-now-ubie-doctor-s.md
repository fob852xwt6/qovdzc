Period cramps and early pregnancy cramps can feel similar, but they differ in cause, timing, and pattern.

Key differences at a glance:

Period cramps are typically sharp, throbbing, and centered in the lower abdomen. They start 1–2 days before your period and ease as bleeding progresses.

Early pregnancy cramps are usually milder, more like a dull ache or light pulling sensation. They often occur around the time of a missed period and may be accompanied by light spotting (implantation bleeding), nausea, breast tenderness, or fatigue.

Duration: Period cramps last 2–4 days. Pregnancy-related cramping tends to be intermittent and short-lived.

Location: Period pain often radiates to the lower back and thighs, while early pregnancy cramps are usually localized to the lower belly or one side.

Explanation

Period cramps and early pregnancy cramps can be confusing because they often feel similar.

Differences:

Cause:

Period cramps are caused by the uterus contracting to shed its lining.

Early pregnancy cramps may occur as the uterus changes to support a growing baby.

Timing:

Period cramps usually happen just before or during a period.

Pregnancy cramps can occur in the early weeks of pregnancy, often before a missed period.

Other Symptoms:

Periods often come with bleeding, while early pregnancy may have light spotting or no bleeding at all.

Pregnancy may also include symptoms like nausea or breast tenderness.
