Metastatic prostate cancer is treatable, though not curable. Standard treatments include hormone therapy (androgen deprivation therapy), chemotherapy, radiation, immunotherapy, and newer targeted therapies such as PARP inhibitors and radioligand therapy. These options can slow disease progression, ease symptoms, and extend survival. Treatment plans are personalized based on cancer stage, genetics, prior therapies, and overall health.

Metastatic prostate cancer is a type of cancer that has spread to other parts of the body. Treating it can involve different methods to help control the disease and improve quality of life.

Hormone Therapy: This is often the first step. It works by lowering testosterone levels, which can slow the growth of cancer.
