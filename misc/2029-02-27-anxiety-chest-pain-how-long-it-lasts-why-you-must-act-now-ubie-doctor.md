Chest pain from anxiety typically lasts only during an anxiety or panic attack, ranging from a few minutes to about an hour. It usually peaks within 10 minutes and gradually fades as the episode resolves. Unlike cardiac-related pain, anxiety chest pain is often sharp, localized, and eases with calming techniques rather than worsening with physical activity.

However, chest pain can have many causes—some serious—and anxiety symptoms can closely mimic heart conditions. Because self-diagnosis is unreliable and delaying care for a true cardiac issue can be dangerous, it's worth getting a clearer picture of your symptoms right now. Explanation

When anxiety causes chest pain, it typically coincides with an anxiety or panic attack. During these episodes, people may feel heart pounding, tightness in the chest, and overall discomfort. This specific type of chest pain usually lasts for just a short while, often ranging from several minutes to about an hour.

Once the anxiety begins to calm down, the chest pain tends to lessen as well. However, sometimes the pain might seem more prolonged because you are very focused on your body's sensations—a kind of hypervigilance that can make an episode feel longer than it is.

It is important to know that if your anxiety-related chest pain lasts significantly longer or comes with other worrying symptoms (like difficulty breathing, dizziness, or severe discomfort), you should call 911 and go to the emergency room.
