Bluntly put, the 488 is a facelifted and turbocharged Ferrari 458, which ended its life cycle after six lovely years. However, the 488 is a far more important car for the Italians. It represents the first forced induction, mid-engined Ferrari since the famous F40 (built from 1987 through 1992), and a glimpse into the future of Ferrari production units.

THE NEW 3.9-LITER twin-turbo V-8 generates a positively monstrous 661 horsepower and 561 lb-ft of twist. It also provides a superb animalistic engine note that is the stuff of dreams. It’s just what you imagine while gazing at a picture of a Ferrari. Allegedly, my tester 488 had a radio, but I cannot confirm that because I only wanted to hear that glorious V-8 singing.

There’s a staggering amount of technology accompanying that engine, including a system to delicately modulate the boost levels at lower revs to keep the turbos spooled and the monumental acceleration linear. Don’t fret over turbo lag. You’ll have the maximum response to stuffing your foot to the floor in a mere 0.8 seconds. You’re still getting a whiplash-inducing crush of power that typically signifies you’re in the hypercar strata. As you mat it, simply grip until your knuckles go white and let your pupils dilate; everything is about to go blurry. Literally.

THE 488 GTB HOWLS to 60 in an eye-watering 2.7 seconds. It’ll rocket on to 100 mph in 6 seconds flat. Want to pass someone? To go from 45 to 65 will take you only 1.2 seconds. It’s hard to describe the sensation of this level of quickness. Car magazines often resort to hyperbole to do so adequately, but none is required for the 488. It is so goddamned fast, it makes your stomach feel funny.

Understandably, to achieve stats like that, you’ve got a ton of oomph pushing to the rear tires. The power is all managed smartly by the second-generation Side Slip Control system, perhaps better known as Ferrari’s drift-happy traction control. The SSC makes sure the surging pushes of the engine translate smartly, instantly adjusting the adaptive dampers, the stability control and the electronic limited-slip differential. It’ll let the butt step out and wiggle around a corner, but you won’t ever feel out of control. The SSC is very much responsible for the 488 imparting a wealth of confidence from the instant you settle into the driver’s seat. And it’s responsible for ensuring that confidence doesn’t cause a casualty.

The V-8 does afford a scooch more practicality at the pump. You’ll average about 19 or 20 miles per gallon with the 488, which is pretty damn respectable for a supercar that costs $355,000 (as tested; $250,000 base). Which brings us to the question you’re wondering: is it worth it?

Abso-fucking-lutely.

BECAUSE THE 458 HAD a ton of drama (and a bit more noise) inherently baked in, it was at once exhilarating and draining to drive. With the 458, you’ve always got to be supremely engaged and on, thinking a few moves ahead. Rewarding to flog? Sure, but also hard to drive at anything less than full attention. The 488 manages to capture the excitement of the 458 but dials back the edge enough to leave you with a razor-sharp supercar that’s quite drivable in any situation. Take it to the track and smear Pirelli streaks all over the asphalt. Or slice through backcountry carvers with ease. Or take it for a 250-mile trip on the highway. Your use case is your choice, and the car will be equally at ease in any setting you place it in. All the while, the Ferrari will flatter you, making you feel like a far better driver than you are.

Enzo Ferrari may have never intended to make vehicles as bright, poised and lethal as the 488 available to the general public, but—thankfully—here we are. And Ferrari himself would have been damn proud of Maranello’s latest stallion in the stable.
